# Positioning — what our films argue

From the competitor review that preceded the 2026-07 films. These are the
arguments the work makes; the craft exists to make them land.

## The stance

**Quiet people-first dignity.** The category's loudest voice (Rippling) sells
with chaos-comedy — screaming founders, absurd escalation, HR as disaster
management. We go the other way: calm, warm, unhurried, treating the workforce
as people rather than as a punchline. That contrast *is* the differentiation;
it stops working the moment we chase the same energy.

## The two things nobody else owns

**1 · The multilingual workforce.** No competitor markets Arabic and Bengali
natively. Our companion answers a warehouse worker in the language they think
in. Show it — the trilingual chat scene exists for this reason and earns its
screen time in every HR film.

**2 · The one thread from application to first payslip.** Competitors sell ATS
or HRIS. We carry a person across both. The "bridge" shot — the dark ATS
candidate card sliding into the warm HR day-one world — is the visual argument,
and it is why both 30s and 45s ATS films end there.

## What the viewer should believe at the end

- HR film — *"this treats my people like people, in their own language."*
- ATS film — *"I would see every candidate clearly, and lose nobody."*
- Either — *"this is a serious, expensive, considered product."*

## What to avoid

- Feature lists read aloud over UI. Features are evidence, not story.
- Comedy, chaos, or a problem-agitation opener with a stressed protagonist.
  That is the competitor's register.
- Claiming "AI". House rule (root `CLAUDE.md`): never the word "AI" in
  user-facing copy — "smart", "intelligent", or better, just describe what it
  does. "Ask the companion anything" beats "AI-powered assistant".
- Stock-footage-only films. Real product, real recordings, or brand-true motion
  graphics. Generated or stock footage is B-roll and connective tissue, never
  the substance.
